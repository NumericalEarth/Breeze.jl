How stability changes
turbulent mixing

A turbulence closure decides how vigorously eddies transport momentum, heat and water—and how much turbulent kinetic energy they retain. Calibration changes those decisions through four stability functions and the mixing length.

1 · Diagnose the environment

The Richardson number compares buoyancy to shear: Ri = N² / S². Negative Ri means unstable stratification. Large positive Ri means buoyancy resists vertical motion strongly relative to shear.

2 · Set the transport

Each diffusivity is K = S(Ri) ℓ √e. A constant closure uses the same coefficient in every regime. A variable-Ri closure can respond differently in unstable, neutral and stable air.

3 · Evolve the energy

Shear and buoyancy produce or remove TKE, while dissipation removes it. Changing a coefficient therefore changes both transport now and the energy available for transport later.

Read the combinations, not just the individual numbers

Neutral local-equilibrium limits, neglecting TKE transport, reveal combinations that can remain similar even when individual coefficients move. They are diagnostic limits rather than imposed constraints.
