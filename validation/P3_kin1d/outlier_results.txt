Parcel matrix outlier diagnosis, 2026-10-15
=========================================

The three outliers have different dominant causes. Identical-state probes
agree on nucleation and immersion-freezing formulas. The evidence identifies
numerical-policy and thermodynamic-convention differences; it does not diagnose
a new production P3 process-code bug. Residual differences remain.

All trajectories on the Breeze side use actual ParcelModel and Simulation/RK3.
No comparison adapter advances them. An adapter is used only for isolated,
identical-state process-rate probes, where dry-air coordinates, constant-cp
heating, and limiter intervals must match upstream to test the formulas.

One comparison-setup bug was found and fixed: the aerosol universal gas constant
was set to 8.314, whereas the pinned upstream source sets rr=8.3145. Both
parcel_compare.jl and reference_conventions.jl now use 8.3145 in their reference
configurations. Production defaults and process formulas are unchanged.
Near the activation target, that small constant error made a probe report zero
activation instead of 6.39893e6 kg^-1 s^-1. After correction, activation agrees
to 1.7e-8 relative. This is a difference of only about 5000 droplets/kg in the
per-step update at dt=0.00078125 s, not a 100% error in the droplet population.

Setup
-----
Cases 3,9,18 from the 18-case matrix. Breeze dt=0.0025 s, fixed process tau=0.025 s;
upstream dt=0.00078125 s. Corrected Breeze runs save every 0.1 s for 5400 s.
Upstream retains full states every 10 s and records process-entry states/rates
every 0.1 s around the events. That close-up has <0.001 s of time staggering
relative to the completed Breeze step. Original matrix data are preserved.

Controlled upstream changes are made ONLY in isolated diagnostic copies. The
unaltered instrumented baseline is bit-for-bit identical to the archived
upstream trajectory. These altered copies must not replace the official
reference or be presented as agreement with unmodified P3.

Case 9: 288 K, RH=100%, w=2 m/s -- Cooper nucleation adjustment
----------------------------------------------------------------
Near 4725 s, ice supersaturation reaches the 5% nucleation threshold. Upstream
uses (Ntarget-Nice)/dt and jumps to the target in one call. Breeze uses the
configured 0.025 s relaxation; the new ice increases vapor depletion, switches
the threshold off, and leads to a much slower increase in number. Both compute
the same Cooper target/rate when given identical states and adjustment times.

Changing ONLY upstream's two Cooper mass/number denominators to 0.025 s:
  Maximum number difference / upstream peak: 55.453% -> 3.104%.
  Final number: Breeze 145512.5; native upstream 227089.5; relaxed upstream
    145460.3 per kg moist air (0.036% final difference from Breeze).
  No material change to the other two outliers.
The late ice-number discrepancy is primarily a finite-relaxation versus
instantaneous-adjustment difference, not an incorrect Cooper formula.

Case 3: 288 K, RH=60%, w=2 m/s -- repeated trace cleanup
------------------------------------------------------
Upstream removes ice below qsmall=1e-14 kg/kg and clears rime when its volume
falls below 1e-15 m^3/kg. Those operations occur every call. At tiny dt, newly
created seeds can be erased repeatedly instead of accumulating. Small rime
also gates secondary ice production (Hallett-Mossop), amplifying the delay.
Breeze retains prognostics and relaxes diagnostic bounds through tendencies.

Changing only the relevant cleanup in separate upstream copies gives:
  Policy                     max |dT| at 10 s   max |dNi| / upstream peak
  Native upstream                    0.30864 K                    70.020%
  Retain positive trace ice          0.26483 K                    57.996%
  Retain positive trace rime         0.13255 K                    25.545%
  Retain both                        0.04340 K                     8.935%

At 0.1 s sampling the last temperature difference is 0.04629 K. The time to
reach 1e-8 kg/kg total ice differs by ~49.5 s with native cleanup and ~1.9 s
when both trace reservoirs are retained. Process activation thresholds and
rime-density bounds are preserved in these diagnostic changes. The large
timestep sensitivity is therefore unsurprising: reducing dt also changes how
much seed survives a per-call cutoff. Residual differences are not eliminated.

Case 18: 300 K, RH=100%, w=2 m/s -- freezing-threshold timing
----------------------------------------------------------------
Near 3579 s, T crosses the immersion-freezing threshold of 269.15 K. A very
rapid glaciation event warms the parcel by several K. The native reference
crosses about 0.3 s later. A few millikelvin of pre-event temperature difference
therefore becomes a large instantaneous difference during the event.

Upstream qv_sat uses epsilon=0.622; its own Rd/Rv is about 0.622197. Breeze's
saturation is consistent with its equation of state and gas constants.
Changing ONLY upstream ep_2 to rd/rv reduces the phase offset and differences:
                                  Native reference     Consistent saturation
  Maximum |dT| at 10 s samples             0.28645 K                 0.01377 K
  Maximum |dT| at 0.1 s samples            2.96282 K                 0.92027 K

The original 10 s output understated this short-lived discrepancy. A remaining
subsecond timing difference still produces a noticeable high-frequency error;
this is not a claim that the two integrations become identical. The identical-
state immersion-freezing rates agree to floating-point accuracy. Truncating
Breeze's gas-constant ratio to match upstream would degrade thermodynamic
consistency and is not proposed.

Validation and limitations
--------------------------
  433 existing identical-state process/projected-update checks passed.
  50 new probe validity/formula checks passed (11 outlier/threshold states).
  55 trajectory integrity/conservation checks passed.
  Baseline instrumentation reproduces the upstream data exactly.
  Only diagnostic upstream copies and benchmark constants changed; no production
  P3 formula, integration method, process timescale, or default changed.

The diagnostic variants establish dominant mechanisms, not full equivalence.
Other probe rate differences, including diffusional growth at roughly 0.2%,
remain and are not claimed to be resolved here. These results support keeping
process-formula tests separate from trajectory agreement under differing
adjustment and clipping policies. Future matrices should report event timing
and adequately sampled trajectories alongside final states and mass errors.
