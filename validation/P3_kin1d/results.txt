P3 / kin1d comparison results — 2026-10-14
========================================

Conclusion: partial agreement; Breeze P3 is NOT certified by this comparison.
The experiments expose material host-model differences, so they also do not
establish that the remaining discrepancies are bugs in P3 process equations.

Nine hour-long column runs completed: six Breeze runs and three kin1d runs.
Every run is finite, has 121 complete output profiles, and passes positivity
checks allowing floating-point roundoff. The matched runs agree initially in
temperature, pressure, vapor fraction, grid, and output times. Both dry controls
retain identically zero water and hydrometeor number throughout.

The dry control is the most important qualification:
  Temperature RMS difference: 2.14184 K
  Maximum temperature difference: 9.53089 K
This exists without any phase changes. The native kin1d driver advects T and
adds -g*w/cp cooling. Breeze transports potential temperature through a fixed
reference-pressure profile. In the continuous dry limit, that gives cooling
proportional to T/T_reference; the two host equations cease to coincide as the
column departs from its initial sounding. Their discrete transports also differ.
Thus the roughly 2 K temperature discrepancy in the cloudy case cannot be
attributed to P3 alone, and it would contaminate any such attribution.

  Fixed aerosol, WENO5         3.04%  24.22%  17.38%    19.25%     81.22%
  Fixed aerosol, upwind        1.59%  23.06%  16.40%    13.31%     68.26%
  Upwind, Flatau, 1 s times    1.62%  18.19%  19.55%    14.47%     66.10%

Errors integrate absolute profile differences over 30–3600 s using the shared
initial density. They are not errors in final column amounts. No acceptance
threshold was fitted to these measurements. The last row changes multiple
options together, so its differences cannot be attributed to one option alone.

Temperature RMS differences in those four comparisons are 1.94193, 1.93734,
2.06049, and 2.04788 K, respectively.

Halving dt from 1 to 0.5 s changes cloud/rain/frozen-ice profiles by:
  Breeze fixed-aerosol run: 0.0533%, 0.1077%, 0.8839%
  kin1d:                   0.8405%, 0.8375%, 0.7301%
Temperature changes by 0.00855 K RMS in Breeze and 0.00474 K in kin1d.
This supports the conclusion that the cross-model differences in these bulk
quantities are not primarily the 1 s timestep error. It is a two-resolution
sensitivity test, not an asymptotic convergence proof. Reference coating changes
by 12.38% on timestep halving, so its comparison remains less well converged.

Supersaturation:
  Max |prognostic - diagnosed| in Breeze fixed aerosol: 2.15153e-4 kg/kg
  Same diagnostic in the adapted reference: exactly zero
The reference explicitly rediagnoses it after each microphysical update;
Breeze uses a transported prognostic with a fixed-time relaxation. The equality
check therefore tests different algorithms and is not by itself a bug test.

The fixed-aerosol comparison is substantially closer than the original example
for rain. Nevertheless, matching advection order, saturation formulation, and
selected relaxation times does not produce quantitative equivalence.

Reproduction is documented in README.txt and automated by run_all.jl. The
study's own environment was successfully instantiated. Runs used Julia 1.12.7,
Oceananigans 0.113.6, CairoMakie 0.15.16, GNU Fortran 16.2.0, and double precision.
Breeze base commit: 6eabb8937670f61ad3bfd0ca2ad400a747681646, with the working-tree
example conversion recorded in the output's breeze_working_tree.patch.
Reference commit: f90df9613fc90360f846d2f12114b967ef3e53ce, P3 5.5.2.
The table-1 checksum matches the upstream table exactly (see README.txt).

To isolate P3 implementation correctness, the next test must remove these host
differences: compare identical thermodynamic states and external tendencies at
the microphysics interface, with transport/sedimentation controlled separately.
This comparison supplies reproducible counterexamples to whole-column numerical
equivalence, not a unique diagnosis or a justification for tuning process rates.
