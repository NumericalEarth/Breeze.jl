Outcome
-------
Breeze's P3 equations agree with direct calls to upstream P3_MAIN when the
thermodynamic conventions and integration policies are matched. No CM1 is used.
The accepted trajectory timestep is 0.1 s, after checking 1 s and 0.5 s.

Single-step tests (12 initial states, dt=1 s):
  35 limited process rates, including condensation/evaporation, activation,
  autoconversion/accretion, deposition/sublimation, riming, melting, shedding,
  heterogeneous nucleation/freezing, and number budgets.
  Non-negligible active rates agree within 0.031%, apart from the explicitly
  documented trace evaporation difference below. Many rates agree to roundoff.
  Maximum temperature difference after the full step: 3.36e-6 K.
  The reference end-of-step particle projection is applied in the comparison
  driver; both projected and unprojected Breeze updates are retained on disk.
  433 agreement assertions pass, including a 0.1% process-rate tolerance.

  All 14 cases use identical acceptance criteria: temperature error <0.001 K,
  state error <0.5% of that variable's reference peak plus an absolute 1e-10
  trace floor, finite/nonnegative states, and closed-parcel water conservation

Timestep sensitivity
--------------------
At 1 s and 0.5 s the cold ascent exhibits abrupt nucleation bursts whose timing
is sensitive to small numerical differences. Upstream itself changes by about
55% in integrated ice-number history and up to 3.04 K when dt goes from 1 to
0.5 s. These coarse steps do not pass the final strict trajectory criteria.
At 0.1 s both implementations agree closely, including the particle-number
history. This establishes implementation agreement at the tested step; it is
not a claim of timestep convergence for every P3 configuration.

Bug corrected in Breeze
-----------------------
StaticEnergyState used reference-temperature latent heats in
  s = cp_m T + gz - L_l q_l - L_i q_i,
while cp_m changes with phase composition. This mixes temperature references.
The offsets must instead be L_0 = L_ref - (cp_v - cp_phase) T_ref, so a phase
transfer releases the temperature-dependent latent heat L(T).

Before the correction, nine independent analytical phase-transfer checks failed.
Near freezing, the old convention under-heated condensation by about 25% and
heated freezing by about 2.7 times its physical latent heat. The corrected state,
static-energy diagnostic, and transported condensate enthalpy are consistent.
A new regression covers condensation, deposition, freezing, and their reverses
at three temperatures in Float32 and Float64 (108 checks).

The ordinary parcel example has been rerun with the correction, and its large
PNG/SVG figures regenerated under validation_output/p3_parcels. Targeted unit,
saturation-adjustment, parcel, sedimentation, one-moment microphysics, moisture
conversion, and P3 regression tests pass; all five explicit-import checks pass.

Remaining distinctions and scope
-------------------------------
The test-only adapter supplies upstream dry-air mixing ratios, saturation formulas
(including the limiter and melting balance), constant latent heat/cp, native
physical constants, and final PSD/trace projection. Production Breeze retains
its moist-air fractions, mixture thermodynamics, and tendency-based constraints.
No production P3 process formula or empirical parameter was tuned to match.
The direct tests therefore validate the tested P3 process paths, not identity of
native Breeze and kin1d host dynamics, sedimentation, aerosol-reservoir treatment,
multiple ice categories, or every possible particle regime.

One upstream edge case is deliberately not copied: at exactly liquid saturation,
the reference leaves a 1.51e-9 kg/kg/s rain-evaporation rate while zeroing other
liquid evaporation terms. Breeze limits the complete evaporation budget to zero.
The rate test explicitly allows 2e-9 only for this case/process; it does not relax
the other rate tolerances. Roundoff-level zero rates use an absolute 1e-15 floor.

A subsequent liquid–ice potential-temperature host comparison and production
example rerun are documented separately in liquid_ice_results.txt. The original
constant-cp reference benchmark above remains useful as an independent P3 test.
